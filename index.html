This plan was built on a comprehensive communications audit, Neighborhood Navigator demographic analysis, 278 congregational survey responses, and two direct-interaction focus groups. It integrates four foundational documents — Brand Book, Ministry Journey Map, Communications Audit, and Neighborhood Navigator — and translates them into a clear, phased, measurable execution strategy across every channel and touchpoint.
Plan Sections
Overview 01
How Growth Actually Happens at Greenhouse
The research reveals a defining growth dynamic: 75% of attendees arrived via a personal invitation from friends or family, while only 9% discovered the church through search or social media. This highlights both the church's greatest strength and its most significant gap. Relational momentum is the primary engine — but Greenhouse is currently under-leveraging the digital channels that could extend that reach to the "unconnected" who are not yet in those relational circles.
Strategic Insight
The flywheel has two sides: Attract more people through a high-trust digital presence, and activate the congregation to invite their "One" with greater frequency. These paths are self-reinforcing — digital discovery provides the validation for relational invitations, and relational trust increases the likelihood that a digital search leads to a Sunday visit.
Real growth occurs where digital presence and relational trust intersect. Neither channel alone is sufficient — the strategy is designed to strengthen both simultaneously.
Overview 02
Core Finding: An Operational Gap, Not an Identity Problem
The core findings from the congregational research were strikingly consistent. People are drawn to Greenhouse for the teaching, the kids ministry, and the clear theology. However, they face significant hurdles when trying to form real friendships, stay in the communication loop, and identify their next steps. This is not a failure of identity — it is a gap in operational scalability. This plan is designed to close that gap.
Two Foundations Built to Bridge the Gap
Brand Book — defines voice, tone, visual identity, and messaging clarity across every channel and touchpoint.
Ministry Journey Map — outlines exactly how people move through the church, from first impression to microchurch participation.
Overview 03
Three-Phase Implementation Timeline
Full rollout is aligned with the University of Florida academic calendar and the natural seasonal reset when families re-engage with church. The strategy is sequenced intentionally: infrastructure before content, systems before campaigns, foundation before scale.
Phases
Phase 1 — Preparation (May–Aug 2026): Build every system, integration, and communication rhythm before the launch window opens.
Phase 2 — Launch (Sep–Dec 2026): All engines engage. Peak awareness and engagement season. Back-to-School Festival, Your One congregation drive, sustained first-time guest growth.
Phase 3 — Expansion (2027): Scale what works. Podcast, TikTok-native content, 200% microchurch growth, full Tier 2–3 event calendar.

Foundation 03
The Strategic Framework — Three Interlocking Engines
This plan is built on three engines working simultaneously, each reinforcing the others. No single system carries the load alone. The combined effect is a growth flywheel that becomes harder to stop once it starts moving.
Three Engines
The Growth Flywheel — Attract → Engage → Delight. Each stage fuels the next. Congregation delight generates advocacy, which powers attraction, which feeds engagement.
The Marketing Funnel — Broad awareness narrows to specific action. Designed to guide strangers through eleven touchpoints across four platforms until they walk through the door on Sunday.
Brand Marketing — Shapes perception over time. Not a campaign — a consistent identity expressed at every touchpoint. Answers the unspoken questions: Can I trust you? Do I fit here? Will this change my life?
Brand 01
The Key Persona — "Grace"
Grace is the single most influential individual within the Key Persona — demographically specific, psychographically representative of the audience most likely to help Greenhouse grow. Speaking directly to Grace creates resonance with the entire target audience. When Grace feels seen, understood, and invited in, others follow.
Grace's Profile
Age 32–44 · Married · 1–2 kids (elementary or younger) · HHI $110K–$210K · Healthcare, tech, education, finance
Values integrity over image, experience over theory, depth over noise. Drawn to Jesus but wary of organized religion. Wants to belong before she has to believe. Wants her questions welcomed, not managed.
Secondary personas: College students (54,000+ within 20 min of campus) and Gainesville Greg — Grace's skeptical husband, open if she's enthusiastic. Reached through men's events, sports, and practical content.
Brand 02
Brand Positioning — Built on Genuine, Not Produced
Greenhouse's brand position is not built on being the biggest, the loudest, or the most produced. It is built on being the most genuine. In a church landscape shaped by performance and polish, Greenhouse's advantage is that it feels real. Every brand decision should reinforce this differentiation rather than erode it.
What GH Is — vs. What It Is Not
Is: An authentic, welcoming church in Gainesville · A community where real people belong · A family you join · Accessible to skeptics and seekers · Rooted in mission and community
Is not: The biggest church in Gainesville · A performance-driven production · Just a place you attend · Insider-language driven · Personality-dependent
Brand 03
Voice & Language System — Six Defining Traits
At least one of Greenhouse's six voice traits must be present in every piece of external communication. These traits are not aspirational guidelines — they are functional filters for every headline, social caption, email subject line, and stage announcement.
The Six Traits
Boldness — Conviction without aggression. The gospel is not hedged.
Everyday Language — Clear, non-churchy, accessible. Anyone should understand it. No Christianese or insider jargon.
Empathy — Creates space for people to be honest about where they are. No judgment, assumption, or pressure.
Authenticity — Doesn't pretend to have it all figured out. We're growing too.
Cultural Relevancy — Speaks to the world people actually live in — their Monday mornings, not just their Sundays.
Hope & Joy — Not forced positivity. A deep, resilient joy grounded in God.
Brand 04
Website Redesign — Designed for Newcomers First
Greenhouse's current site serves insiders well but creates distance for first-time visitors. The homepage leads with a sermon clip and mission statement — both of which resonate with believers but signal an insider experience to seekers. The redesign inverts this: every page is built for the person who doesn't yet know if Greenhouse is for them.
Priority Redesign Areas
Homepage hero: Lead with "Is this for me?" — not the mission statement. Real people, warm moment, one clear CTA.
"I'm New" page rebuild: Opens with the emotional reality of a first visit, not movement theology. One next step: Plan Your Visit form.
Navigation restructure: I'm New first · Sundays · Connect · Kids & Youth (elevated) · About · Give
Photography standard: Real Greenhouse people in real moments. Stock imagery actively undermines the belonging brand.
Attract 01
The Awareness Ecosystem — Cumulative Trust, Not Single Touchpoints
Grace's path to a first visit is rarely linear. It looks like this: a friend mentions the church, she notices a social post, she Googles Greenhouse, reads a few reviews, sees another post, hears it at a community event, gets invited again — and eventually decides to show up. No single channel closes the decision. The impact is cumulative. The strategic goal is not to optimize any one moment in isolation — it is to ensure that across all of them, the experience is consistent, credible, and warm.
Key Channels
Word of Mouth / Your One — Highest-trust entry point. A friend she trusts opens the door.
Social Media (organic) — Builds brand awareness and culture. She sees who GH is before she decides to visit.
Google Search + Grants — Captures active intent. She's searching for a church — GH appears. Up to $10K/month in free search advertising via Google Ad Grants.
Community Events — Non-threatening first contact. She attends before she attends a Sunday.
Podcast — Deepest trust-builder pre-visit. She hears Pastor Mike before she steps foot in the building.

Digital Marketing — $10K Annual Budget + $120K Google Ad Grants
The paid digital budget of $10,000 is allocated across four channels. Independently, Google Ad Grants provides up to $10,000/month — $120,000/year — in free search advertising, representing more than 10× additional reach at zero cost. A well-managed Grants account can generate 800–1,500 additional website visitors per month from high-intent searches, translating to 24–45 additional first-time guest inquiries monthly at no added cost.
Budget Allocation
$2,500 — Boosted Post Social Advertising · Taking GH social content to a wider audience
$2,500 — Event-Specific Promotion · Paid campaigns for Tier 1 and Tier 2 events
$2,500 — Inbound Marketing · SEO support, landing pages, felt-need keyword campaigns
$2,500 — Reserve / Testing / Outsource · A/B testing, experimental campaigns, overflow
Attract 03
Social Media Strategy — A Consistency Problem, Not a Quality Problem
Over 91 days, Greenhouse posted 53 times on Instagram and 56 times on Facebook — roughly every other day, with gaps. When compared against two Assembly of God churches with similar weekly attendance, Greenhouse fell 25–35% behind in every metric category. The gap is not a content quality problem — it is a consistency and volume problem. The fix is daily posting on both platforms plus $50/week in strategic post boosting.
Platform Priority & Content Pillars
Instagram (primary) — Daily Stories, 4–5 Feed/Reels per week. TikTok cross-posted simultaneously.
Five content pillars: Community Life · Teaching & Insight · Behind the Mission · Invitation & Next Steps · Hope & Encouragement
Target: 80%+ increase across all primary social metrics (views, viewers, content interactions) within 90 days of sustained execution.
Attract 04
Pastor Mike as a Marketing Asset
People follow people before they follow institutions. A church led by a pastor who is genuinely present in social content will consistently outperform one that only communicates at an institutional level. Trust forms faster through relationship than branding. Institutions create awareness. People create trust. And trust is what drives attendance.
Priority Actions
60–90 second sermon clips — Captioned and formatted for social. Highest-performing content type for church accounts.
Podcast — Extended teaching accessible before someone visits. A trusted voice in someone's earbuds builds relationship without requiring a Sunday commitment.
TikTok (Phase 4) — 1–2 direct-to-camera posts per month. Unscripted, direct. The platform's algorithm gives zero-follower accounts 100,000+ reach on a single strong video.
Attract 05
"Your One" — Congregation Activation
"Your One" is not a program — it is a culture shift. Every person at Greenhouse is holding a seat for someone who isn't there yet. The question is simple: who is your one? Think of one person in your life who needs what Greenhouse offers. Pray for them. Invite them. And when they come, be the reason they feel expected, not just welcomed. Done well, this is not a push — it's permission.
Launch & Integration
Launch moment: One Sunday dedicated to casting vision from stage with a real life-change story. From that point forward, Your One is always active.
Every channel carries it: Social stories · newsletter inserts · event campaigns · website landing page · blog posts written for the person being invited · GH Guide perforated insert cards
Target: 60%+ of first-time guests should report being personally invited via the FTG form.
Events 01
Why Events Matter — The Low-Stakes On-Ramp
Not everyone's entry point is a Sunday morning. Many people — especially Grace and Greg — need a lower-stakes on-ramp. Before they attend a service, they want a sense of who Greenhouse is and whether they can imagine themselves there. A well-designed event does three things at once: builds momentum in the community, gives the congregation something natural to invite their One into, and creates a clear pathway into Sunday mornings and the broader assimilation process.
At Every Event
Visitor forms and QR codes · coffee gift card incentives · Greenhouse Guide distribution · a simple next step communicated before people leave. Nothing passive. Nothing accidental.
Events 02
Tier 1 — Large Community Events (500–2,000+)
Tier 1 events are designed to brand Greenhouse publicly and collect visitor information at scale. Three anchor events are planned across the year, each with a 4–6 week promotional campaign and dedicated visitor follow-up automation launched within 24 hours.
Tier 1 Calendar
Fall Back-to-School Festival (Aug/Sep) — Outdoor festival on campus. Inflatables, food trucks, live music, kids activities. Fall is the single most strategic moment in the church calendar for reaching families. $750 paid promotion.
Christmas Event (December) — $750 paid promotion to Gainesville area outside GH database. Christmas-specific email sequence launched within 24 hours.
Spring Community Event (Mar/Apr) — Easter Experience or outdoor family event. $750 paid promotion. Same visitor capture and follow-up protocols.
Events 03
Tier 2 & 3 — Community & Ministry Events
Tier 2 events (50–300 attendees) give the congregation something specific and natural to invite their One to. Tier 3 events serve spiritual development while creating space for a congregation member to bring a friend into a lower-commitment experience first.
Event Menu
Tier 2 — Family Movie Night · Splash Party · Lego/STEM Family Challenge · Community Pickleball Tournament · Expert Night: Protecting Your Kids Online · Expert Night: College Prep for Families
Tier 3 — Women's Conference · Men's Conference · Marriage Conference · Marriage Retreat · Men's Grill-Off or Game Event (designed specifically for Greg)
Engage 01
The Five-Stage Assimilation System
On average, people will interact with a church — both digitally and in person — for approximately seven hours before deciding whether to stay or move on. Every additional connection point shortens that timeline and reduces back-door risk. The goal is simple: remove ambiguity early, and replace it with repeated, low-friction experiences of connection.
Stages
Stage 1 — First Visit: Personal text from staff same day · FTG Email #1 Monday. Goal: thank, welcome, invite back.
Stage 2 — Nurture: 4-email FTG sequence over Days 1–19. Goal: introduce GH, highlight ministries, events, Activate.
Stage 3 — Pastoral Bridge: Personal invitation to Pastor Coffee / Welcome Lunch. 80% of attendees complete Activate within 30 days. This is the highest-leverage step in the entire system.
Stage 4 — Activate: Dedicated sequence connecting to microchurch, serving, and giving.
Stage 5 — Active Member: Transition to ongoing discipleship communications. Microchurch, serving, tithing, kingdom building.
Engage 02
Expanding Community Beyond Sunday
One of the clearest findings in the research is the difficulty people experience forming meaningful, lasting relationships at Greenhouse. There is currently an over-reliance on microchurches as the primary relational on-ramp. The solution is not to replace microchurches — it is to broaden the ecosystem of connection points at lower commitment levels. Belonging rarely starts at depth. It starts at proximity, repetition, and shared context.
Additional Connection Points
Affinity Groups — Running clubs, parents of teens, book clubs, sports leagues. Low-barrier relational on-ramps where trust builds naturally, often before spiritual conversation begins.
Family Hangouts — Potlucks, park days, game nights. Unstructured relational space that prevents quiet disengagement between Sundays.
Bible Studies — Mid-week structured options that create a second weekly touchpoint with the church.
Engage 03
Sunday Experience — The First Moment of Truth
The First Moment of Truth is when someone visits for the first time and decides whether to lean in or pull back. All digital marketing, social media, word-of-mouth, and events that bring someone to their first Sunday are pre-FMOT work. The Sunday experience itself is the delivery. The 15 minutes after service ends are the highest-leverage conversion window in the entire first-visit experience.
Critical Touchpoints
Pre-arrival: Confirmation email with parking map and kids check-in info. Campus signage that eliminates confusion from road to seat.
In-service text capture: Pastor invites everyone (not just guests) to text a keyword for a local coffee gift card. Removes social awkwardness and dramatically increases capture rate.
Post-service 15 minutes: Pastor Mike visibly accessible in lobby. First-time guest hosts assigned to spot and engage. The personal coffee invitation extended to every identifiable first-time guest.
Comms 01
The Communication Gap — and Why It's Relational
The current newsletter reaches 971 email addresses within a congregation of 2,050+ weekly attendees. This gap represents an important opportunity for growth in connection and alignment. Closing this gap is not just an operational improvement — it is a relational one. When people are consistently in the communication loop, they are more likely to stay engaged, take next steps, and remain connected to community. In a distracted culture, "out of sight" leads directly to "out of mind."
Comms 02
Two Weekly Newsletters — Different Audiences, Different Purposes
The communications architecture runs two distinct weekly email lanes, with 8 additional monthly ministry newsletters. Each lane has a defined audience, frequency, and purpose — not overlapping, not competing.
Weekly Lanes
Thursday — Church Update Newsletter (full congregation): What's happening Sunday, upcoming events, ministry announcements, ways to serve, community wins. Warm, brief, conversational — feels like it was written by a person who loves the church.
Tuesday/Wednesday — Discipleship Email (full congregation + active FTGs): Sermon-based blog post or pastoral reflection in Pastor Mike's voice. Personal, direct, real. Not a recap — an insight or challenge that stands on its own.

Monthly Ministry Newsletters
Kids Ministry · Youth · College · Women's · Men's · Microchurch · Missions · Serving — each segmented to the right audience, each with a defined content focus.
Comms 03
Blog Strategy — One Post Per Week, Sermon-Anchored
The blog is both a discipleship tool and an SEO engine. Every post opens with the human problem before introducing Scripture — Grace should feel seen in the first paragraph. The blog also serves the Your One strategy: 1–2 posts per month are written specifically for the person being invited, not for the existing congregation.
Format
One post per week tied to the Sunday sermon series · Written in Pastor Mike's voice · Ends with 3 reflection questions and a clear next step · Published Monday/Tuesday for the mid-week email · Different angle used for each platform (not copy-paste)
Comms 04
The Greenhouse Guide — Highest Shelf-Life Piece GH Produces
Published 2–3 times per year, the magazine lives in homes for weeks, gets shared with friends and family, and reinforces Greenhouse's identity every time someone picks it up. Unlike any digital touchpoint, it demands physical attention. It is the single piece in the communication system most likely to end up in the hands of someone who has never attended.
Structure & Cadence
Fall Edition (Aug/Sep) — Aligned with Back-to-School Festival · Spring Edition (Jan/Feb) — New year energy · Optional Summer Edition (May/Jun)
Content split: 70% evergreen (who we are, Activate, ministries) + 30% seasonal (current series, events, life-change stories)
Your One insert: Each Guide includes a dedicated perforated invite card that congregation members can hand directly to their One.
Data 01
The Infrastructure That Prevents People From Becoming Invisible
Marketing gets people in. Communications keeps them engaged. But the system that prevents people from quietly slipping out — without anyone noticing until it's too late — is the data infrastructure. Greenhouse's transition to PushPay and a unified ChMS is not an IT upgrade. It is the foundational operating system for everything else in this plan. When this system is weak, people do not disappear intentionally — they simply become invisible.
The Core Principle
The system alerts — a person follows up. Technology makes detection possible, but the intervention is always human. A personal phone call or text from a pastor or ministry leader is what closes the back door.
Data 02
Engagement Scoring System
Every person in the ChMS is scored based on a combination of attendance, serving, giving, microchurch participation, and Activate completion. The score determines what communication they receive and when a pastoral alert is triggered.
Engagement Levels
New Contact — First visit only → FTG sequence (4 emails over 19 days)
Visitor — 2–4 Sunday visits → Ongoing FTG + Activate nurture
Regular — Weekly/bi-weekly attendance, 1–2 other indicators → Standard discipleship communications
Active — 5+ qualifying activities in 60 days → Transition to discipleship + microchurch invitation
Core — Microchurch + serving + giving + Activate complete → Delight stage — life-change stories, advocacy tools
Drifting — Previously Regular/Active, no activity in 30+ days → Drift alert to staff · personal outreach triggered immediately
Data 03
Closing the Back Door — Drift Detection Triggers
Disengagement rarely announces itself. People don't leave churches loudly — they quietly stop showing up. The drift detection system is designed to catch early signals before someone has already mentally moved on.
Alert Thresholds
Attendance drop: No check-in for 3 consecutive weeks → Personal text or call from ministry leader
Kids check-in stops: Previously active family, no check-in for 4 weeks → Campus pastor personal outreach
Giving lapse: Consistent giver stops for 4+ weeks → Pastoral notification. Giving lapses often signal broader disengagement.
Email disengagement: No opens in 60 days → 3-email re-engagement sequence · if no response, move to low-frequency list
Roadmap 01
Sequencing Principle — Stability Before Scale
This plan is designed to be built in three phases over 12 months. The sequencing is intentional: foundation first, then momentum, then acceleration. Trying to run all channels simultaneously before the underlying infrastructure is in place will dilute effort, waste resources, and produce inconsistent results. Stability must come before scale.
